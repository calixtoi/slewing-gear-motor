{# ── Supplier speed calculation alert ────────────────────────────────────── #}
Common supplier misunderstanding — output speed calculation

Suppliers sometimes calculate gearbox output speed by dividing motor speed by the slewing ring ratio — which gives the wrong result:

❌ Wrong calculation (supplier's scope confused with slewing ring):
1,400 RPM / 150 = 9.3 RPM — uses slewing ring ratio
1,400 RPM / 121 = 11.6 RPM — uses slewing ring ratio
These ratios belong to the slewing ring, not the gearbox.
✓ Correct calculation (using supplier's own gearbox ratio):
1,400 RPM / 38 = 37 RPM — uses i_gb = 38 (PF-XXL example)
1,400 RPM / 28 = 50 RPM — uses i_gb = 28 (another example)
Target window: 30 to 60 RPM at the gearbox output shaft.

Why: The transmission chain has two separate gearboxes in series. The supplier's helical bevel gearbox (ratio ≈ 27–40) delivers 30–60 RPM to the slewing ring input shaft. The slewing ring worm (ratio 121:1 or 150:1) then applies a further reduction to produce the final crane boom speed of 0.25–0.50 RPM. These are two completely separate ratios belonging to two completely separate components.

≥ 3.0Required minimum for offshore crane duty

Why ≥ 3.0? Offshore conditions include voltage drops from generator supply (−10%), cold temperatures (−20°C), salt/moisture in windings, and high bearing stiction at start. Each factor reduces effective starting torque. A k_start of 3.0 ensures M_a still exceeds M_req after all derating.
