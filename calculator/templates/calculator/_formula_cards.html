1Worm shaft torque — maximum

{% latexify f1 math_block=True %}

Purpose: The crane's maximum slewing torque acts at the worm wheel output shaft. To find what the motor and bevel gearbox must deliver at the input of the worm stage, we divide by the worm gear ratio and multiply by the efficiency inverse. A low worm efficiency (e.g. 40%) means the motor must produce 2.5× more input torque than the worm output torque — this is the dominant sizing factor for offshore crane drives.

{# Step 3 #}

3Required gearbox catalogue torque

{% latexify f3 math_block=True %}

Purpose: Bevel gearbox catalogue ratings are given as nominal torque (T2N). We multiply M₂,nom by a safety factor of 1.34 to account for dynamic shock loads, application factor, and temperature — per standard bevel gearbox selection methodology. The selected gearbox must have T2N ≥ M_gear,req. Example: if M₂,nom = 386 Nm → M_gear,req = 517 Nm → choose a gearbox rated ≥ 760 Nm.

4Bevel gearbox ratio

{% latexify f4 math_block=True %}

Inverse — output speed from ratio (datasheet gives i):

{% latexify f4_inv math_block=True %}

Purpose: The bevel gearbox bridges the motor (high speed, ~1450 rpm) to the worm shaft input (lower speed, e.g. 43 rpm). This ratio is not a free choice — it is determined by dividing the motor's nameplate speed by the required worm input speed. The result must match an available gearbox ratio (e.g. 33.4:1). When the datasheet gives the actual gear ratio i directly, use the inverse form to compute the output speed.

{# Step 7 #}

7Available motor starting torque

{% latexify f7 math_block=True %}

Purpose: At standstill (locked rotor), an IEC induction motor produces its maximum torque — the starting torque M_a. This is expressed as a multiple of the rated torque M_n via the ratio k_start = Ma/Mn. The ratio is given on the motor datasheet. High-torque motors (e.g. Class D) can achieve Ma/Mn up to 4.0+, allowing a smaller rated power motor to be used where starting conditions are demanding.

VariableMeaningTypical value
M_nMotor rated (nameplate) torque [Nm] — torque at rated speed and rated power4–25 Nm
k_start = Ma/MnLocked-rotor torque / rated torque ratio — from motor datasheet. Higher is better for starting heavy loads.2.0–4.0
M_startActual starting torque the motor produces at standstill [Nm]15–80 Nm
{# Step 8 #}

8Torque feasibility check

{% latexify f8 math_block=True %}
{% latexify f8c math_block=True %}

Purpose: The margin is the ratio of available starting torque to required starting torque. A margin of 1.0 means the motor is exactly on the limit — any voltage drop, temperature rise, bearing stiction, or manufacturing tolerance will cause it to stall. The 1.3× minimum ensures safe start under adverse conditions typical for offshore equipment (generator supply, cold temperatures, salt/moisture exposure). Margins above 1.1 are acceptable but below 1.3 is not recommended for offshore cranes.

ResultMeaning
≥ 1.30OK — Motor suitable for offshore crane duty
1.10–1.29Marginal — Not recommended; acceptable in last resort
< 1.10Fail — Motor too small; will stall at peak load

GB3Load spectrum ratio λ

{% latexify fg3 math_block=True %}

λ represents the "heaviness" of the duty cycle. λ = 1.0 means the crane always runs at maximum torque (very heavy). λ → 0 means it is rarely loaded. Typical offshore crane: λ ≈ 0.35–0.55.

VariableMeaning
λDimensionless load spectrum ratio = nominal ÷ maximum torque at worm shaft

Supplier Data Check Criteria

Torque & Power margins

{% latexify fs_margin math_block=True %}

Purpose: Checks that the supplier's stated torque or power value is at least 10% higher than the calculated minimum requirement. The 1.10 threshold (10% margin) accounts for manufacturing tolerances, measurement uncertainty, and future re-use of the motor in a slightly different configuration.

≥ 1.10 OK — adequate margin
≥ 1.00 Marginal — within spec but no margin
< 1.00 Fail — insufficient, motor too small

Applied to: motor starting torque, motor rated torque, motor power, gearbox torque rating.

Ratio deviation check

{% latexify fs_ratio math_block=True %}

Purpose: Gear ratios must match the design values closely to achieve the target slewing speed. A deviation above 2% would result in a noticeably different crane speed or require re-selection of the gearbox series.

≤ 2% OK — ratio matches calculation
> 2% Mismatch — wrong gearbox selected

Applied to: bevel gearbox ratio, worm gear ratio.
